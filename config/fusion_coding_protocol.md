# Fusion-level coding protocol

This protocol is a review template. It does not retroactively validate the labels
in the original workbook.

## Unit of analysis

Use one included WoS record, identified by `UT`, as the primary unit. If a paper
evaluates multiple materially different fusion architectures, retain the paper as
one bibliographic record and record a primary and, where necessary, secondary
fusion level. Do not silently count one paper more than once in percentage totals.

## Inclusion in the denominator

A record may enter the fusion-level denominator only when:

1. it belongs to the frozen final corpus;
2. the reviewed method actually integrates two or more sources, modalities,
   representations, models, or decisions under the study's stated fusion scope;
3. sufficient methodological information is available to assign a level; and
4. the rule for handling hybrid methods has been applied consistently.

Records lacking sufficient evidence should be labelled `unclear`, not forced into
a fusion category. Their inclusion or exclusion from the denominator must be
reported explicitly.

## Operational categories

- **Data-level:** observations are combined before learned representation
  extraction, for example co-registered channel or image stacking.
- **Feature-level:** learned or engineered intermediate representations are
  combined before the final prediction.
- **Decision-level:** independently produced scores, probabilities, labels, or
  maps are combined.
- **Hybrid:** fusion occurs at two or more analytically distinct levels and cannot
  be represented faithfully by a single primary level.
- **Unclear/not applicable:** evidence is insufficient, or the study does not meet
  the operational definition of fusion used for the percentage analysis.

## Evidence and quality control

For each record, store the quoted or paraphrased methodological evidence and its
location. Prefer two independent coders for ambiguous cases and record the final
adjudication. Report category counts and the denominator before percentages.

The retained working materials contain 163 records with archived paper-level
assignments, including 131 feature-level-only records. The other 98 records in the
261-record bibliometric corpus do not have complete auditable assignments. Because
the archived subset was not generated through a prospective or representative
sampling design, its category counts are reported only for supplementary auditing
and are not used to estimate the distribution for all 261 records.
