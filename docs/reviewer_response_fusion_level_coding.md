# Reviewer response: paper-level fusion coding

> **Response:** Thank you for requesting clarification of the paper-level coding
> underlying the reported feature-level fusion percentage. We have revised the
> Methods and Results sections to distinguish the complete bibliometric corpus from
> the subset used for fusion-level analysis.
>
> All 261 records remained in the bibliometric corpus and contributed to
> publication, author, journal, institution, country, keyword, citation, and
> co-citation analyses. Fusion-level coding constituted a separate manual,
> paper-level content analysis. During revision, we audited the retained working
> materials and restricted the reported fusion-level distribution to 163 records
> with archived paper-level assignments. For these records, the abstract, author
> keywords, dataset information, and reported model description were examined,
> with the location of information integration within the classification pipeline
> serving as the principal coding criterion. The occurrence of the terms
> “fusion” or “ensemble” in a bibliographic record was not, by itself, treated as
> sufficient evidence of a classifiable fusion architecture.
>
> The original working materials did not retain a complete auditable fusion-level
> assignment for the other 98 records. We therefore did not reconstruct those
> assignments retrospectively. These records were excluded only from the
> fusion-level percentage denominator; they were not excluded from the
> bibliometric corpus. The revised manuscript now states explicitly that the
> resulting percentages describe the archived coded subset rather than all 261
> records.
>
> After rechecking and standardising the retained coding labels, 131 of the 163 coded
> studies were coded as feature-level only (80.4%), 18 as hybrid (11.0%), seven as
> data-level only (4.3%), and seven as decision-level only (4.3%). We have therefore
> corrected the previously reported value of 80.9% to 80.4% and now report the
> complete numerator, denominator, and category distribution. We have also
> clarified that these frequencies describe the archived coded subset and do not
> establish the technical superiority of feature-level fusion.
>
> To make this result auditable, we now provide a record-level supplementary
> workbook for all 163 archived assignments. It contains the WoS accession number,
> DOI, title, publication year, model, dataset and fusion-method notes retained in
> the working sheet, the original label, the standardised fusion category, and the
> operational basis code. Formula-based summary cells reproduce 131 feature-level,
> 18 hybrid, seven data-level, and seven decision-level assignments. We do not
> claim that this table is an exhaustive coding of all 261 bibliometric records.
